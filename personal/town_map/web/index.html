Finding a town to settle down

Built for those deciding where to settle down in Massachusetts

Schools come first. They matter if you have or plan to have kids, and strong schools tend to go with nice neighborhoods, good amenities and lower crime. Treat them as a signal, not a guarantee.

How to use it

  1. Shortlist towns with strong schools (shaded green or purple). Tap the star on a town's details to save it, then compare or download your list in the Saved tab.
  2. Check the commute: tap the train button on the map to show commuter rail and T lines, or use the ruler for driving time.
  3. Check home prices and taxes in the town's details, then search listings on Redfin.

School score

The average of three rankings for the town's public school district. Each one ranks the district against every Massachusetts town from 0 to 100:

  • MCAS: grade 10 average scaled score across subjects
  • AP: share of AP exams scored 3 or higher
  • SAT: average reading/writing + math score

Scores are relative, not absolute: 70 means better than about 70% of towns. Towns in a regional district share its score. Towns with missing test data have no score.

Other numbers

Typical 3-bed home
Zillow Home Value Index for 3-bedroom homes (middle third of the market), latest month. The change compares it with the same month a year earlier.
Property tax rate
Residential tax rate for the latest fiscal year each town has set, shown as a percent of assessed value.
College-bound
Share of the district's high school graduates enrolled in college the fall after graduating.
High school size
An estimate: the district's grade 10 MCAS test-takers × 4.
Density
Population ÷ town area. Rural under 200 people/sq mi, sparse suburban 200–499, mid-dense 500–999, dense suburb 1,000–3,000, urban city over 3,000.

Sources

  • MA DESE Education-to-Career data hub (MCAS, AP, SAT, college enrollment)
  • Zillow Research (home values)
  • MA Division of Local Services (tax rates)
  • U.S. Census ACS 5-year (population)
  • MBTA GTFS (commuter rail and T lines)
  • OSRM (driving distances for the ruler)

Each source is pulled at its latest available release whenever the data is rebuilt.
